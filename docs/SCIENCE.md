# Science and interpretation

## Supported framing

- Smith et al., PLOS ONE (2022), [The phytochemical diversity of commercial Cannabis in the United States](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0267498). The findings support questioning broad retail labels as descriptions of chemical diversity. They do not validate Flower Spectrum's specific ten-profile taxonomy or parameter values.
- Oswald et al., ACS Omega (2023), [Minor, Nonterpenoid Volatile Compounds Drive the Aroma Differences of Exotic Cannabis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37901519/). This supports the boundary that common terpene panels omit compounds important to aroma. The model is an interpretation of measured inputs, not the complete smell.

## Model assumptions

The inherited 38-compound engine assigns primary and supporting categories with normalized contribution weights and per-compound multipliers. Examples include linalool 1.2 and farnesenes 0.3. These are model parameters, not established odor thresholds. Gas/Fuel includes an inherited balance heuristic; it does not measure volatile sulfur compounds. Dessert/Creamy is an interpretation category rather than a separately measured compound.

“Fingerprint” denotes a batch-linked visual summary. Different panels can produce similar summaries; the visualization is not an authentication mechanism, genetic identifier or guarantee of uniqueness.

## Extraction gates are not sensory accuracy

The 0.02 percentage-point tolerance checks agreement between included source rows and the reported total. The 95% modeled-mass gate quantifies support for the extracted mass. Neither is a calibrated probability that the aroma classification is correct, and neither independently validates a laboratory report.
