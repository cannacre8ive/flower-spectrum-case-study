The problem

Months of care.
One tiny label.

A premium cultivator’s work should be easier to understand than a strain name and a potency number.

Genetics, cultivation and handling produce a complex plant. Yet the information people encounter is often fragmented: a lab PDF in one place, a label in another, and a fresh explanation at every counter.

Flower Spectrum’s design premise is simple: give that complexity a consistent visual language. Preserve the measured data, make the interpretation readable, and carry it into every format.

Why look beyond broad labels? A 2022 study found commercial categories did not consistently reflect chemical diversity. Read the research.

The science, in plain language

Think of a
recipe for smell.

The lab lists some ingredients.
The model organizes the notes.
The fingerprint draws the pattern.

◌

What is a terpene?

A small plant-made molecule. Some help give lemon peel, pine needles and many other plants their familiar smells. A report tells us how much of each tested compound was measured.

≋

Why mix the notes?

A recipe is more than its biggest ingredient. The model gives each measured terpene a weight, splits its contribution among aroma groups, and adds the contributions together.

✳

How do I read it?

Each colored section always means the same aroma group. A longer section has a larger relative model score. Smaller sections keep the supporting notes in the picture.

Change an ingredient.
Watch the pattern change.

This teaching example starts with more myrcene than linalool. Floral can still lead because the model assigns different weights. Move a slider to see the logic.

This is a transparent interpretation model. Its weights and ten categories are design assumptions, not validated measurements of smell intensity. Other aroma compounds matter too; the fingerprint does not predict effects. Explore the science and its limits.

The builder behind the system

The skill is
connecting the parts.

Spencer Gray brings domain knowledge, creative direction and AI-assisted engineering into one coherent product.

This project connects document extraction, explicit data rules, a visual identity, reusable layouts and print output. The design work reaches into the data model; the engineering work reaches all the way to the finished artifact.

AI supported implementation and iteration. In the original suite, OCR helps read scanned reports. Classification itself uses versioned rules, so the same inputs produce the same result. Human review remains the handoff between extraction and use.

What success means

Clearer information.
Less drift.

The case for the project is inspectable behavior, not invented business results.

  • Source rows reconcile before the record is approved.
  • Every generated specimen carries the same batch ID and fingerprint.
  • Scientific measurements remain distinct from interpretation.
  • The explanation works without specialist vocabulary.
  • The print handoff preserves the visual identity.
