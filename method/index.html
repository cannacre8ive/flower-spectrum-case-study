The science & the design model

A map of the notes.
Not the whole song.

A lab report measures selected compounds. Flower Spectrum turns that panel into an understandable visual pattern. Those are two different jobs.

Start simple.

Imagine a recipe card.

The lab lists some ingredients and their amounts. Flower Spectrum sorts them into familiar aroma groups and draws a picture of the mix. Just as a recipe card cannot tell you exactly how a meal will taste, this picture cannot tell you everything about a flower’s smell.

Same ten positions, different relative lengths. Fictional study panel.

Before classification

Three checks.
Then a human decision.

Read the right number.

Choose the result column, not the detection limit. Convert mg/g into percent by dividing by ten. Keep original labels, units and result strings so a person can inspect the extraction.

Reconcile the source.

Sum included source rows and compare them with the reported total. The current software tolerance is 0.02 percentage points. This is an extraction check, not laboratory certification.

Disclose the coverage.

The model must cover at least 95% of the extracted mass. Unknown compounds remain visible. This threshold is a product safeguard; it is not 95% accuracy at predicting aroma.

A person confirms the review before a new record is approved. Missing units, ambiguous aliases, scanned pages and unfamiliar report layouts require attention. This case-study walkthrough supports its bundled text-PDF fixtures; the original suite also includes reviewed OCR.

Inside the classifier

Measured amount.
Explicit assumptions.

Amount × model weight × profile share = contribution

The model has 38 supported compounds and ten fixed aroma categories. Each compound contributes to a primary category and sometimes supporting categories. Contributions add up; normalized scores determine the fingerprint.

For example, the inherited linalool weight is 1.2. That is a chosen model parameter, not a measured odor threshold. Flower Spectrum does not present these weights as a validated sensory or machine-learning model.

What about Gas / Fuel and Dessert / Creamy?

They are interpretation categories. The code includes a co-occurrence bonus for balanced caryophyllene, limonene and myrcene/humulene. That heuristic does not detect sulfur compounds or establish a chemical cause of fuel aroma. Dessert is also a composite descriptor, not a single measured “dessert molecule.” These assumptions need sensory validation.

Research behind the framing

Evidence, with boundaries.

Smith et al. · PLOS ONE · 2022

Chemistry is richer than broad retail categories.

An analysis of commercial cannabis samples found that Indica, Hybrid and Sativa labels did not consistently align with observed chemical diversity. This supports exploring more informative descriptions; it does not validate Flower Spectrum’s specific categories or weights.

Read the original study ↗
Oswald et al. · ACS Omega · 2023

Terpenes are only part of aroma.

Researchers identified important aroma differences associated with minor nonterpenoid compounds, including volatile sulfur compounds. A common terpene panel can miss meaningful sensory information. That is why the fingerprint is framed as an interpretation of a measured subset.
